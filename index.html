Designing pathways for ions

Materials architecture for safer, longer-lasting metal batteries.

Functional nanofibers → Continuous ion transport → Stable interfaces → Safer metal batteries
01 · OVERVIEW

My research sits at the intersection of materials chemistry and electrochemical energy storage. I use one-dimensional inorganic nanofibers, polymer matrices, bio-inspired structures, and interfacial engineering to connect microscopic transport mechanisms with practical battery performance.

02 · RESEARCH PROGRAMS
01

Nanofiber-reinforced solid polymer electrolytes

Ultralong lithium-containing hydroxyapatite nanofibers form robust, continuous pathways that improve Li+ transport and stabilize lithium-metal interfaces.

02

Multi-channel ion transport

Dielectric and superionic conductors act as active components that regulate polymer-chain motion, local electric fields, and interconnected transport channels.

03

Bone-inspired sustainable hydrogels

Zinc-doped hydroxyapatite nanofibers and gelatin combine rapid Zn2+ transport with water retention, mechanical integrity, recyclability, and biodegradability.

04

Electrode and interface architectures

Substrate chemistry, current distribution, and conductive carbon networks are studied to understand lithium deposition and stabilize silicon anodes.

03 · MATERIALS & METHODS
Inorganic nanofibers Hydroxyapatite conductors Polymer & gelatin matrices Electrochemical analysis Interface characterization Mechanism-guided design
04 · NEXT DIRECTIONS

Fiber-based solid and gel electrolytes for metal batteries; advanced separators and functional fibrous membranes; and scalable manufacturing routes that translate nanofiber architectures into deployable energy-storage components.

KEYWORDS · solid-state batteries · aqueous zinc batteries · nanofibers · hydroxyapatite · ion transport · electrode interfaces
